### iPhone and the Environment

**iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max are designed to reduce environmental impact:22**

[See the iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max Product En­viron­men­tal Report (PDF)](https://apple.com/environment/pdf/products/iphone/iPhone_17_Pro_and_iPhone_17_Pro_Max_PER_Sept2025.pdf)

**Progress towards Apple 2030**

- Apple 2030 is our commitment to be carbon neutral for our entire carbon footprint. Our plan to decarbonise products is rigorous and focuses on transitioning to low‑carbon electricity, designing with recycled and low‑carbon materials, and prioritising lower‑carbon ways of shipping products, like sea freight.

[See Apple’s commitment](https://apple.com/uk/environment/)

**Materials**

iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max are made with 30% recycled content,23 including:

- 50% recycled aluminium in the enclosure
- 100% recycled cobalt and 95% recycled lithium in the battery
- 100% recycled gold plating and tin solder in all Apple‑designed printed circuit boards
- 100% recycled gold wire in all cameras and gold plating in all connectors
- 100% recycled tungsten and copper wire in the Taptic Engine
- 100% recycled copper in the vapour chamber enclosure
- 100% recycled rare earth elements in all magnets

**Energy**

- 40% of manufacturing electricity for iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max is sourced from renewable electricity24
- Exceeds US Department of Energy requirements for battery charger systems25

**Packaging**

- 100% fibre-based packaging26
- iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max packaging is designed to be more efficient than iPhone 16 Pro boxes, increasing the number of devices shipped per trip by 2527

**Waste**

- No established final assembly sites generate waste sent to landfill as part of Apple’s Zero Waste Programme28

**Smarter chemistry**29

- All the materials used in Apple products, accessories and packaging are covered by the requirements of our [Regulated Substances Specification](https://apple.com/environment/pdf/Apple_Regulated_Substances_Specification.pdf), which was one of the first in industry to restrict the use of key substances of concern. See the latest [En­viron­men­tal Progress Report](https://apple.com/environment/pdf/Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025.pdf) to understand Apple’s recent efforts in phasing out these chemistries.
